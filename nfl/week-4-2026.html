What this board looks like

No double digits, anywhere

Week 3 produced an 11.5 and a 9.5. Week 4 tops out at 8.5, with Chicago hosting the Jets and Baltimore hosting Tennessee.

That is a market with no strong convictions about any matchup on the board. Eight of sixteen games sit at three points or fewer, which continues the pattern Week 3 established: the obvious gaps have been priced, and what remains is close.

Five divisional games

Pittsburgh at Cleveland, New England at Buffalo, Kansas City at Las Vegas, the Chargers at Seattle, and Atlanta at New Orleans. That is nearly a third of the slate.

Divisional games historically finish closer than the number suggests. Teams that meet twice a season know each other's personnel, tendencies and coaching habits intimately, and familiarity compresses margins. None of these five is priced above 5.5, which suggests the market already knows it.

Pittsburgh at Cleveland opens the week with a total of 40.5 — the lowest on the board. A cold-weather divisional game on a short week is exactly where points go to disappear.

London at 9:30 in the morning

Indianapolis and Washington meet at Tottenham Hotspur Stadium at 9:30 AM ET on Sunday, the third international game of the young season after Melbourne and Rio.

The handicapping considerations repeat, and they matter every time. Neither team is at home, so conventional home advantage is neutral. Both squads have travelled, both have lost a normal preparation week to logistics, and both are playing at an hour their bodies do not recognise as football time.

What is different here is the total: 50.5, the second-highest on the board. The market expects points from a game where both teams are tired and displaced, which is a view worth noticing rather than assuming.

Three weeks in: what has actually been established

By Week 4 the sample is large enough to be suggestive and still too small to be conclusive. Three games against three different opponents in three different settings is genuinely more informative than one, and it is not a season.

What has changed since Week 1 is that the market now has evidence rather than projection. The tight spreads across this board are not uncertainty in the Week 1 sense — they are a market that has looked at the evidence and concluded these teams really are close.

What to look at this week

  • The short week. Pittsburgh and Cleveland played Thursday, meaning compressed preparation for a physical divisional game. It usually shows in execution rather than in the final margin.
  • Post-international recovery. Teams returning from overseas games have historically found the following week difficult. Worth remembering when Baltimore and Dallas appear again after Rio.
  • Injury accumulation. Four weeks of football produces a real injury list, and reports become more informative as the season settles.
  • Totals in cold-weather venues. Buffalo, Cleveland and Chicago are all hosting. October is not December, but the direction of travel on those totals is downward from here.

Reading the numbers on this page

Line shows the favourite and the points they must win by. Total is the combined score expected from both teams, and you bet whether the result finishes above or below it.

The half points prevent ties — a team cannot win by three and a half, so -3.5 cannot push while -3 can. With eight games on this board at three points or fewer, that distinction decides a great many bets. Full mechanics: how to read betting lines.
