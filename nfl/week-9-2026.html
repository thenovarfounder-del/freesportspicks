Madrid: the Bernabéu hosts football

Cincinnati and Atlanta meet at the Santiago Bernabéu at 9:30 AM ET — the seventh international fixture of the season, after Melbourne, Rio, three in London and Paris.

The usual considerations apply: neither team is at home, both have travelled, both lose a preparation week to logistics, both play at an hour their bodies do not recognise.

What is different is the number. The total sits at 49.5, well above the 42.5 the market put on the Paris and Wembley fixtures. After progressively marking these games down through the season, it has priced this one as a normal game.

Across seven international fixtures the totals have run 48.5, 51.5, 50.5, 45.5, 42.5, 42.5 and now 49.5. There is no simple rule — the market prices the teams, not just the travel.

Seattle -13.5, and Arizona's month

Seattle hosts Arizona laying 13.5, matching the largest number of the season. It is also the third time in four weeks Arizona has been a heavy underdog — 13.5 at the Rams in Week 6, 7.5 at home to Denver in Week 7, 10.5 at Dallas in Week 8.

And it is a divisional game, which continues to be where the biggest numbers keep appearing this season despite the historical pattern of divisional matchups finishing tighter than the spread.

What a run of big spreads tells you

A team repeatedly priced as a double-digit underdog is one the market has genuinely written off. That is information, and it cuts both ways: the number reflects real assessment, and a market with a settled view is a market that can be slow to update when something changes.

The rest of the board

Dallas at Indianapolis carries 52.5

The highest total of the week, indoors at Lucas Oil. The pattern has been consistent all season — domes carry higher totals, because wind and precipitation are removed as variables and the market prices that difference explicitly.

Two games at 41.5

The Jets at Kansas City and Cleveland at New Orleans share the lowest number on the board. In the Superdome that is notable: an indoor game priced this low is the market rating the offences, not the conditions.

Green Bay at New England at 1.5

The tightest line of the week, and a non-divisional game between conferences. When the market cannot separate two teams that never play each other, it is usually because it rates them genuinely close rather than because it lacks information.
