Understanding NFL Point Spreads

The point spread exists to level the playing field and attract equal action on both sides of a game. NFL lines reflect public perception not always true team strength. When public perception is wrong and a popular team is overvalued there is a betting edge. Finding those edges every week is what we do.

Key Concept: A team can be better than its opponent and still not cover the spread. The spread is about margin not about who wins. A 3-point favorite that wins by 2 is a loss against the spread even though they won the game outright.

NFL Totals Strategy

The public overwhelmingly bets overs because people want to see scoring. This bias creates value on unders in specific situations. Best under spots: cold weather outdoor games below 32 degrees, games with wind over 20 mph, games featuring two top-10 defenses, and rivalry games where defense dominates.

NFL Moneyline Strategy

If you back a +150 underdog you need to win 40 percent of those bets to break even. If your research says that underdog wins 45 percent of the time in that situation you have a profitable bet even though you lose more often than you win. This is positive expected value betting and it is the only sustainable long-term approach.

NFL Parlay Guide

  • 2-team parlay - True odds 3-1, books pay 2.6-1. House edge roughly 10 percent
  • 3-team parlay - True odds 7-1, books pay 6-1. House edge roughly 12 percent
  • 4-team parlay - True odds 15-1, books pay 12-1. House edge roughly 20 percent

Limit parlays to 2 teams maximum. Flat betting single games beats parlays over any significant sample size.

Frequently Asked Questions

What NFL bets have the best value?

Single-game point spreads and totals at standard -110 juice offer the best value. The house edge is lowest on these bets compared to parlays and teasers.

How do NFL spreads work?

The spread gives points to the underdog and takes points from the favorite. A -6.5 favorite must win by 7 or more to cover. A +6.5 underdog must lose by 6 or fewer or win outright to cover.

What time do NFL lines open?

Lines for the following week typically open Sunday night or Monday morning after that week's games finish.
