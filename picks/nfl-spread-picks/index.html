How to Read NFL Point Spreads

Reading NFL point spreads is essential for any football bettor. The point spread is a number set by oddsmakers that represents the projected margin of victory. When you see a line like "Kansas City Chiefs -7 vs. Denver Broncos," the minus sign indicates that Kansas City is favored to win by 7 points. To win your bet on the Chiefs, they must win by 8 or more points. Conversely, betting on Denver +7 means the Broncos can lose by up to 6 points and you still win your wager.

The standard juice or vigorish on NFL spreads is typically -110, meaning you must risk $110 to win $100. This built-in commission is how sportsbooks generate revenue. Sometimes you'll see reduced juice at -105 or even pick 'em odds at -100, which offers better value for bettors. Understanding these fundamentals is crucial before exploring our NFL ATS picks where we break down against-the-spread betting opportunities.

Components of a Point Spread

  • The favorite: Indicated by a minus sign, the team expected to win
  • The underdog: Indicated by a plus sign, the team expected to lose
  • The spread number: The margin the favorite must cover to win the bet
  • The juice: The commission charged by the sportsbook, usually -110
  • The hook: A half-point added to prevent pushes (ties)

NFL Line Movement and What It Means

Line movement refers to changes in the point spread from when it opens until game time. Understanding why lines move is critical for serious NFL bettors. Sharp bettors and professional handicappers often bet early, causing lines to move based on where the smart money lands. Conversely, public betting typically comes in closer to kickoff, sometimes moving lines in the opposite direction.

Several factors influence line movement including injuries, weather conditions, betting volume, and sharp action. When a key player is ruled out, you'll see immediate line movement reflecting the team's decreased chances of covering. Weather reports indicating heavy wind or rain can also shift totals and spreads significantly. Tracking these movements throughout the week provides valuable insights into where the smart money is positioned.

Types of Line Movement

Steam moves occur when multiple sportsbooks move their lines simultaneously in the same direction, typically indicating sharp action from professional bettors or syndicates. Reverse line movement happens when the line moves opposite to the betting percentages, suggesting sharp money is contradicting public opinion. For example, if 70% of bets are on the favorite but the line moves toward the underdog, this signals respected money on the dog.

Monitoring line movement helps identify value opportunities. Our experts track these movements throughout the week, incorporating this intelligence into our best bets today selections to ensure you're getting the most favorable numbers possible.

Closing Line Value: The Gold Standard

Closing line value (CLV) is widely considered the most important metric for long-term betting success. CLV measures the difference between the line you bet and the closing line when the game begins. If you bet the Cowboys -3 and the line closes at -5, you gained 2 points of CLV. Studies have consistently shown that bettors who regularly achieve positive CLV are profitable long-term, even if individual bets lose.

Professional bettors obsess over CLV because it indicates you're beating the market. The closing line is the sharpest number because it reflects all available information and maximum betting volume. Consistently beating the closing line demonstrates superior handicapping ability and timing. This is why our experts emphasize not just picking winners, but getting the best available number.

Strategies for Achieving Closing Line Value

  • Bet early when you identify an edge before the market adjusts
  • Shop multiple sportsbooks for the best available lines
  • Monitor injury reports and team news before they become public
  • Identify sharp sportsbooks that move quickly and follow their movements
  • Track your CLV over time to measure your handicapping effectiveness

Key Numbers in NFL Spreads

Key numbers in NFL betting represent the most common margins of victory in football. The two most critical key numbers are 3 and 7, which align with field goal and touchdown values. Historically, approximately 15% of NFL games land exactly on 3 points, while about 9% land on 7 points. This makes these numbers extremely valuable when betting spreads.

Understanding key numbers prevents costly mistakes. The difference between getting -2.5 versus -3.5 is massive because crossing that key number of 3 dramatically changes your probability of winning. Similarly, there's significant value difference between -6.5 and -7.5. Smart bettors never give up key numbers without receiving appropriate compensation in the form of better juice.

The Complete Key Numbers Hierarchy

Beyond 3 and 7, other important NFL key numbers include 10 (field goal plus touchdown), 6 (two field goals), 14 (two touchdowns), and 4 (touchdown minus extra point). Secondary key numbers like 1, 2, 8, 9, 13, and 17 also appear more frequently than random numbers. When handicapping games, positioning yourself on the favorable side of these key numbers provides mathematical edges that compound over a full season.

Our handicappers factor key numbers into every recommendation, ensuring you're never unnecessarily exposed to increased risk. This attention to detail separates professional analysis from recreational betting.

Buying Half Points Strategy

Buying half points (also called buying hooks) allows bettors to move the point spread in their favor by paying extra juice. For example, you might buy a half point to move from -3.5 to -3, or from +2.5 to +3. The cost is typically 10 cents of juice, changing -110 odds to -120. While this seems expensive, buying through key numbers can be mathematically profitable.

The strategy of buying points should be employed selectively, primarily when crossing key numbers like 3 and 7. Buying from -3.5 to -3 reduces your risk significantly because you now win on a 3-point victory instead of pushing. However, buying from -4.5 to -4 provides minimal value since 4 isn't a prominent key number. The additional juice cost outweighs the benefit.

When to Buy Points

  • Buying from -3.5 to -3 or from +2.5 to +3 (crossing the most important key number)
  • Buying from -7.5 to -7 or from +6.5 to +7 (crossing the second most important key number)
  • Buying from -10.5 to -10 in certain game situations
  • Teaser bets that naturally move through key numbers for reduced juice
  • Never buying through non-key numbers unless getting special reduced juice promotions

Frequently Asked Questions About NFL Spread Picks

What does "covering the spread" mean in NFL betting?

Covering the spread means the team you bet on performed well enough to win your wager. For favorites, this means winning by more than the spread number. For underdogs, it means either winning outright or losing by fewer points than the spread. If you bet Patriots -7 and they win 24-14, they covered the 10-point margin exceeds 7.

How often should I expect to win NFL spread bets?

To break even at standard -110 juice, you need to win approximately 52.4% of your spread bets. Professional bettors who maintain 55-58% win rates over large sample sizes are considered highly successful. Anything above 60% long-term is extremely rare and indicates elite handicapping ability. Consistency and volume matter more than individual game results.

Should I bet NFL spreads early in the week or wait until Sunday?

Both strategies have merit depending on your approach. Betting early allows you to capitalize on soft opening lines before sharp action moves them, potentially gaining closing line value. Waiting until Sunday ensures you have complete injury information and weather forecasts. Many successful bettors use a hybrid approach, betting some games early when they identify clear edges and waiting on others pending additional information.

What's the difference between NFL spread picks and moneyline picks?

Spread picks require the team to win by a specific margin or stay within a certain deficit, while moneyline picks simply require your team to win outright. Spreads typically offer -110 odds on both sides, while moneylines have varying odds based on the teams' relative strength. Heavy favorites might be -400 on the moneyline but only -9.5 on the spread, offering different risk-reward profiles for bettors.

How do I know if I'm getting good value on an NFL spread?

Value exists when your handicapped probability of covering exceeds the implied probability of the odds. At -110, you need 52.4% probability to break even, so any edge above that represents value. Tracking closing line value, comparing your numbers to market consensus, and maintaining detailed records of your predictions versus outcomes helps identify when you're consistently finding value in the market.
