What Does NCAAB Mean?

NCAAB stands for National Collegiate Athletic Association Basketball, specifically referring to men's college basketball at the Division I level. The NCAA is the governing body that oversees college athletics in the United States, and Division I represents the highest level of competition. NCAAB encompasses over 350 teams across 32 conferences, including powerhouse leagues like the ACC, Big Ten, Big 12, SEC, Big East, and Pac-12.

The NCAAB season runs from early November through early April, culminating in the iconic NCAA Tournament, commonly known as March Madness. Throughout the regular season, teams compete for conference championships and tournament seeding, creating compelling storylines and betting opportunities nearly every day. Understanding NCAAB basketball is essential for anyone looking to successfully bet on college hoops, as the landscape differs significantly from professional basketball with unique rules, playing styles, and competitive dynamics.

How to Bet on NCAAB Basketball

Betting on NCAAB basketball offers numerous wagering options beyond simply picking the winner of each game. Understanding the different bet types available helps you maximize opportunities and find the best value. The most common NCAAB bets include point spread wagers, moneyline bets, and totals (over/under), but many sportsbooks also offer team totals, first half lines, second half lines, and player props.

Point spread betting is the most popular way to wager on college basketball. The spread represents the number of points the favorite must win by to cover the bet, while the underdog can lose by fewer points than the spread or win outright. Moneyline bets simply require picking the winner regardless of margin, with odds adjusted based on each team's perceived chance of winning. Totals betting involves wagering whether the combined score will be over or under a number set by oddsmakers.

Successful NCAAB betting requires proper bankroll management, line shopping across multiple sportsbooks, and disciplined bet selection. Never wager more than you can afford to lose, and avoid chasing losses with increasingly large bets. Our experts recommend risking no more than 1-3% of your total bankroll on any single game. Line shopping means comparing odds at different sportsbooks to ensure you're getting the best price, as even half-point differences can significantly impact long-term profitability.

Using KenPom and BartTorvik for NCAAB Picks

Advanced analytics have revolutionized college basketball analysis, and two rating systems stand above the rest: KenPom and BartTorvik. These sophisticated metrics provide objective measurements of team quality that go far beyond won-loss records. Our expert panel incorporates data from both systems when formulating our NCAAB picks today, using them as foundational tools in the handicapping process.

KenPom, created by Ken Pomeroy, measures team efficiency on both offense and defense while adjusting for strength of schedule and game tempo. The system assigns each team an adjusted offensive efficiency rating (points scored per 100 possessions against average defense) and an adjusted defensive efficiency rating (points allowed per 100 possessions against average offense). These tempo-free metrics allow for accurate comparisons between teams with vastly different playing styles.

BartTorvik, developed by Bart Torvik, offers similar efficiency-based metrics with additional layers of analysis including player-level data, lineup statistics, and predictive modeling. Both systems excel at identifying team quality independent of record, helping handicappers spot overvalued and undervalued teams. A team with a 15-10 record but elite efficiency metrics may actually be stronger than a 20-5 team with worse underlying numbers, creating betting value.

Our experts use these systems to project game outcomes, estimate appropriate point spreads, and calculate expected totals. When our analysis reveals significant differences between the betting lines and what KenPom or BartTorvik projections suggest, we've identified potential value bets. However, we never rely solely on these metrics, always combining them with situational analysis, recent performance evaluation, and expert judgment. What's the difference between spread picks and moneyline picks?

Spread picks predict whether a team will cover the point spread, meaning the favorite must win by more than the spread or the underdog must lose by less than the spread or win outright. Moneyline picks simply predict which team will win the game regardless of margin. Moneyline bets on favorites require risking more money to win less, while underdog moneylines offer larger payouts but lower probability of winning.
